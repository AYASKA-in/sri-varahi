The problem

The corner shop is losing its customers to apps.

Quick-commerce apps run their own dark stores. The kirana store that knows your family for twenty years has no way to take your order on the phone, and no way to compete on visibility.

Today

  • Big apps decide which products you see
  • Shops pay commissions or are left out entirely
  • Money goes through the platform first
  • Riders race the clock

With Sri Varahi

  • Every nearby shop is seen, by design
  • The shop sets its own price and keeps its customers
  • Customers pay the shop directly: cash or the shop's own UPI
  • Delivery in slots the customer picks, by the shop's own riders
Our idea

One app. Many shops. Each one stays the boss.

We give every shop a complete online store, inside one app customers already trust.

01

Shops near you

Customers see every live shop that delivers to their door, open ones first, nearest first, and tap into any of them.

02

One shop per basket

A basket holds one shop's items, so one shop packs it and one rider brings it. Switching shops is a clear, one-tap choice.

03

Check delivery first

On any product, type a pincode or pick a saved address: the app says which store delivers, the next free slot and the delivery charge.

Our ideology

A fair feed: no shop is left out.

Most marketplaces show whoever pays or sells most. Our home feed takes turns: every shop's best offer, then every shop's second best, and so on. Inside a turn we weigh real sales, ratings, nearness and savings against MRP, with a lift for shops that had few orders this week and a daily rotation so first place moves around.

For shop owners

The same tools a big chain has, for the shop on your street.

The name

Why "Sri Varahi"?

Our story began with Sri Lalitha, a family rice and flour brand selling through its own stores. In tradition, Varahi leads the forces of Goddess Lalitha: a protector who stands with the many. That is the role we want for the platform: standing behind every small shop, so the neighbourhood economy stays in the neighbourhood.

Questions

Frequently asked

Who gets the money when a customer pays?

The shop. Customers pay by cash on delivery or straight to the shop's own UPI ID. Sri Varahi never holds the money.

What does a shop pay to join?

Nothing during the free pilot: the first shops get the full app free for the first few months, with up to two stores per business.

Who delivers the orders?

Each shop's own riders, in delivery slots the customer picks. A 4-digit PIN confirms every delivery.

Which languages does the app speak?

English, Telugu, Odia and Hindi.
